World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026

Russia, Ukraine and the US ended a second day of US-mediated talks in Abu Dhabi, their first direct talks in nine months. US envoy Jared Kushner said a prisoner swap of 1,000 to 1,500 per side was discussed, to be finalised Thursday, but there was no breakthrough: territory remained the hardest issue (Reuters, BBC).
Iran's Revolutionary Guards said tankers caught fire after explosions in the Strait of Hormuz; the UAE's ADNOC confirmed only one vessel damaged in its waters. France blamed an IRGC cyber unit for hacking about 700 supermarket fridge systems and summoned Iran's ambassador, even as President Trump called the Iran war "militarily over" (Reuters).
Leaders opened the two-day COP31 summit in Antalya, Turkey, where UN chief Antonio Guterres warned the climate crisis is "outrunning us"; the US stayed away for a second year (AFP).
The Ebola outbreak in DR Congo passed 11,000 confirmed cases across eight provinces, with 5,300 deaths and no licensed vaccine for its Bundibugyo strain.
US consumer prices rose 0.4% in October, beating the 0.3% forecast; the S&P 500 fell 0.38% and Brent crude rose 1.1% to $92.50. The House voted 211-204 to send a bid to impeach Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to committee, effectively ending it (Reuters).
Indonesia's Vice President Gibran Rakabuming Raka said he would resign and had given his letter to President Prabowo, an adviser told Reuters. Malaysia's ex-PM Najib Razak left prison for house arrest after four years over 1MDB (Reuters).
Spain held its first general strike in 14 years, over housing, hitting transport and health care (BBC). Fog pile-ups on Bulgaria's Trakia motorway near Yambol killed at least 11 people (BTA).
Israel's new Knesset was sworn in without a government, Gadi Eisenkot first to try to form one. Baird cut Nvidia's price target 60% in a downgrade citing China chip curbs (Reuters). Pope Leo XIV drew hundreds of thousands to Mass in Cordoba, Argentina.
